## 1 · A child is never sent a link — SCRUM-215

### What the invitation table actually held

The ticket asked for existing student invitation rows to be reported before
anything was done with them, and specifically whose address each held. Six
existed. Here they are:

| Child | `invited_email` held | `parent_contact` held | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Probe Student | *empty* | *empty* | pending |
| Probe Student | *empty* | *empty* | joined |
| Active Control | *empty* | *empty* | joined |
| Bankole Adesuwa | a real address | **a different** real address | joined |
| Ice Root | a real address | **a different** real address | pending |
| Ice Roots | a real address | **a different** real address | pending |

**The answer to the question in the ticket:** on a student row, `invited_email`
did **not** hold the parent's address. The parent's was in `parent_contact`,
separately. So the column held a third thing — in all three cases a team
member's own address, used while testing.

Every one of the six was created by us, between 8 and 30 September. None came
from a school. All six addresses belong to the team, and no parent address was
ever stored under a column that says `invited_email`.

They are already gone — they were in the database that was reset last night,
and the dump that preserves them is at
`backups/pre-reset-20261001-234008.sql`. So there was nothing left to delete
and the report above is the whole of it.

### What changed

`invited_role` no longer accepts a student value — it is teacher only. Every
door a stale student row could still reach refuses it with
`409 student_not_invitable` rather than accepting and quietly doing nothing:
the invite, the resend, and both halves of the join link. A caller that has
not caught up fails where somebody can see it.

The parent consent link is untouched. It is tokenised per parent-and-child
pair and remains the only link that goes out on a child's behalf.

### One thing this cost

Yesterday I built two fields for Olayinka specifically to support an
invite-link child: the invitee's name on the join inspection, and an age on
the join request. Under this ticket the age field has nothing behind it and
comes out; the name survives because invited teachers are still real. I have
told him. That is a day's work on a path that was then ruled out of existence,
and the ordering was mine to get wrong.

## 2 · Nobody but the child sets a PIN — SCRUM-216 with SCRUM-179

### What was there

`POST /students/{id}/pin/reset` generated four random digits, saved them as the
child's PIN and **returned them to the adult who asked**. So an adult both
chose a child's credential and knew it.

Olayinka had closed one of his own items on the strength of that endpoint, on
the basis that admin resets issue four digits and report the length. It was
working as built; it should not have been built.

### What it is now

`POST /students/{id}/pin/clear` — a clear, not a reset. It never accepts a PIN
value, never returns one and never generates one. There is no longer a code
path by which an adult can learn or choose a child's PIN.

- **Open to a teacher**, scoped to the classes that teacher actually takes,
  not the whole school. It used to be administrators only, which meant the one
  person who can recognise the child could not help them.
- **Logged** against whoever did it, with the child and the time. The residual
  risk is a teacher clearing a PIN and setting one themselves on the tablet
  before the child reaches it. Nothing in an API can prevent that, so the log
  is what makes it visible rather than silent.
- **The child sets the new one.** `POST /student-entry/pin` takes the school
  code, the child's Student ID and the PIN they choose. It only opens while
  the PIN is already cleared, so it cannot be used to overwrite a classmate's
  credential — the window exists because an adult who recognised the child
  deliberately opened it, and it closes the moment a PIN is set. Gated on
  consent and the age check like every other door a child can reach.

That last endpoint was a gap I found rather than one that was specified: a
teacher could clear a PIN, and the child then had nowhere to set a new one.
The only set-PIN door in the product was on the entry-token path, which never
resolved for anybody.

### Why it is not self-service

Worth recording, because it will be asked again. A reset needs proof that the
person asking owns the account, and nothing available to a child supplies
that. No email, by design. No phone. Security questions and enrolment facts
both fail because a classmate knows the answers and admission numbers are
frequently sequential. So any self-service reset is also a route into a
classmate's account — and a child signed in as a classmate for twenty minutes
teaches the engine from somebody else's behaviour, which breaks the product
quietly and surfaces nothing.

The teacher is not choosing a credential. They are answering the one question
a machine cannot answer about a child with no email: whether this is really
her.

### On PIN length

Four digits is the length. Six is still accepted **at sign-in only**, so
nobody already on one is locked out, and the response tells the client to make
them set a new one. The length now travels with the PIN — it rides on the
clear response as well as the session — so no screen has to hardcode it.

## 3 · The client stops marking children — asks B8, B9, B14, B27

Four of Olayinka's high asks were one problem wearing four hats. The device
was marking children's answers against an answer key it held, and the result
fed measures of the child: the baseline vector, the scaffold ladder, mastery.
A measure of a child decided on the child's own tablet is not a measure.

**The baseline can now arrive as trials.** `POST /api/baseline/trials` takes
what actually happened — the dimension, the condition, the child's response
and the time it took — and does the arithmetic here. Accuracy, mean and median
response time, and the per-condition breakdown. Where a trial names an item
from the probe bank, it is marked here against the stored answer and whatever
the client said about correctness is ignored.

The median matters: one slow trial from a child who looked away drags a mean
of twenty and says nothing true. On a pre-reduced vector that distinction was
not available to us at all.

The trials themselves are not kept. The reduction is what the engine reads,
and holding six hundred raw trials per child earns nothing. The older reduced
endpoint still works, because the app has been sending it since before the
rule and breaking it would lose baselines rather than improve them.

**The warm-up stopped shipping its own answer key** earlier in the week. The
prompt used to return the answer alongside the question; the child's pick now
goes to the server and is marked against a key that never leaves it.

**Scaffolded practice takes the pick, not the verdict.** The scaffold log took
`responseCorrect` from the device, and that one field fed the scaffold ladder.
It now accepts the child's actual answer and the lesson segment it came from,
and marks it against the calculation stored on that segment. Where no answer
exists on this side — some practice problems are still generated on the
device — the client's verdict is still taken, because refusing the attempt
would lose the evidence, which is worse.

**Raw touch stays on the device**, which is a decision rather than code. Tap
coordinates and per-element dwell are a finer behavioural record of a child
than anything else we keep, and we would be storing it continuously for every
child. What the engine needs from touch is already derivable, and the two
cases that were being misread as idleness — a refused tap, and the system
owning a wait — now have event types of their own. The privacy layer refuses
those field names mechanically, so the rule holds whatever anybody builds
next.

The one part of B8 still open is serving the baseline's knowledge questions
for the subject a child picked. They are currently a hard-coded list that
ignores the choice, and the fix depends on the probe bank in SCRUM-175.
